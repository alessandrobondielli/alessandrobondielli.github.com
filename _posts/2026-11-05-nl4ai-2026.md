The ninth edition of the **Natural Language for Artificial Intelligence (NL4AI)** workshop took place in Perugia on October 7–8, 2026, co-located with the 24th International Conference of the Italian Association for Artificial Intelligence (AIxIA 2026). I had the pleasure of co-organising it with Giovanni Bonetta and Elisa Leonardelli (Fondazione Bruno Kessler) and Irene Siragusa (University of Palermo). The workshop is supported by the AIxIA Special Interest Group on NLP and by the Italian Association of Computational Linguistics (AILC).

The proceedings are now published as [CEUR Workshop Proceedings Vol-4315](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4315/), together with our overview paper:

- [NL4AI 2026: Overview of the Ninth Workshop on Natural Language for Artificial Intelligence](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4315/xoverview.pdf)

## Some numbers

- **26 submissions** from 80 distinct authors in nine countries (Italy, France, India, Poland, Indonesia, the United States, Brazil, Turkey and the United Kingdom).
- 3 papers were withdrawn and **14 were accepted** (acceptance rate 60.9%), involving 63 distinct authors.
- Single-blind peer review, with at least two reviews per paper.

## What the papers are about

The overview groups the accepted papers by theme:

- **Evaluating LLMs.** A multilingual study of the complementarity of LLM-based metrics for retrieval-augmented generation, and an election-based approach that aggregates LLM rankings of system outputs with voting rules.
- **Behaviour and internal representations.** An analysis of chain-of-thought traces for commonsense causal reasoning in the Qwen3.5 family, and a study of how sparse autoencoder activations relate to sentence complexity.
- **Data and task adaptation.** Pseudo-labeled news datasets under a limited LLM annotation budget, and a comparison of scale, specialisation and architecture for Italian fact verification.
- **Clinical applications.** Aligning free-text disease descriptions to ICD-9/10/11 with semantic retrieval, and the prosody of clinical speech (silences and filled pauses) in French psychiatric interviews.
- **Multilingual and low-resource NLP.** LLM translation from Old Occitan into Italian, and cross-lingual few-shot transfer for Indonesian misogynistic meme detection.
- **Responsible language technology.** Gender-counterfactual biographies to uncover occupational stereotypes, annotation-role alignment in emotion recognition, and the resilience of text watermarking under back-translation attacks.
- **Multi-agent workflows.** MAWGE, a library to generate and simulate synthetic multi-agent workflows.

Taken together, the papers stress explicit evaluation assumptions, carefully built resources, and analyses that go beyond a single aggregate score. They separate model scale from task suitability, knowledge availability from retrievability, and observable output from the processes or perspectives it is meant to represent.

Our own group also contributed to the programme: [*Right for the Right Reasons? A Chain-of-Thought Analysis of Commonsense Causal Reasoning*](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4315/paper10.pdf) (Auriemma, Miliani, Bondielli, Passaro and Lenci) is in the proceedings.

Thanks to everyone who submitted, reviewed and attended, and to my co-organisers. See you at the next edition!
